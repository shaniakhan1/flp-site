## Email 2: day 2
Subject: The post is probably in your camera roll
Preview: A useful place to start before you film anything new.

Before you spend an hour looking for a trending sound, open your camera roll.

Look for something that shows how your business works. The prep before opening. A detail you checked twice. The difference between a finished job and a job you’re proud to put your name on.

Then explain why it matters to the customer.

“Here’s our team working” is a caption. “This is the part we check before we call a job finished” gives someone a reason to pay attention.

Pick one image or clip. Write the sentence you’d say if a customer were standing beside you. That’s enough to start.

## Launch checklist
- Verify dedicated segment, consent, double opt-in, sender authentication, mailing address, unsubscribe, and client exclusion.
- Publish PDF and landing page, then replace staged form placeholder with verified hosted form or narrowly allowed embed.
- Test with one authorized internal address: confirm, receive delivery, download, inspect links, unsubscribe, confirm suppression.
- Test existing subscriber does not accidentally enter other-brand workflows.
- Activate only after delivery and suppression succeed; do not launch broad broadcasts.
- Homepage placement: below services; secondary navigation link “Free planner”. No entry popup competing with paid-service inquiry.
- Record form views, confirmed opt-ins, planner clicks, inquiry submissions, qualified calls, signed clients. Do not treat opens as reliable intent. Review by signup cohort; no invented targets or lift claims.

## Monthly founder note

Working title: A note from Shania. One email per month to the separately opted-in FLP founder-note segment. Plain layout, 200-350 words, one current observation grounded in Shania’s actual experience, one usable idea, one question inviting a reply. No invented personal anecdotes or automatic recycled trend claims. Draft each issue for review before sending; scheduling remains unconfigured.

Use Gmail for individual conversations and qualified-lead replies. Use the subscription platform for opted-in monthly distribution and automated delivery, with Shania as the visible sender and replies returning to her business inbox. Confirm the connected Gmail address before configuring routing. Never use a bulk BCC list as a substitute for unsubscribe management.

First issue concept: “Your business doesn’t need to go viral to be worth talking about.” Source material: Shania helped bring local informer attention to her brother’s bagel shop; visitors created videos; franchise had its own marketing team. Do not invent sales, visits, dates, or creator fees. Angle: relevant local attention can be useful even when a business has an existing marketing team. Practical exercise: identify three local creators by city, audience fit, and quality of local recommendations, then draft a specific invitation with clear expectations and any compensation.
